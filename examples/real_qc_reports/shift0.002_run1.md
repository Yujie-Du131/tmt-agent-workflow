# QC report on quantms output (shift0.002, run 1, model `claude-sonnet-5-5`)

**Overall:** major_issues

Major problem: channels ch2 (TMT127) and ch4 (TMT129) are essentially unquantified (1 and 4 proteins vs about 466 in other channels), and the extreme medians and z-scores are artefacts of those few proteins. As a result the correlation metric is NaN throughout and the spike-in slopes were computed on only four channels. The metrics show the dropout; the cause is inferred. The most plausible suspects are the very tight reporter_mass_shift (0.002 Da), a possible m/z offset affecting the 15N-based 127/129 reporters, or a design-file channel mapping problem. The remaining four channels look consistent with each other (n_quantified 465-468, raw medians 20.26-20.59), and spike-in slopes (0.74-1.16) show no clear failure but are only partially informative.

### 1. [major] ch2, ch4
- Observation: Two of the six TMT channels (ch2 = TMT127, ch4 = TMT129) have almost no quantified background proteins, while the other four channels each have about 465-468. The channels are effectively unquantified. Their raw_median values (11.4 and 13.2) and z-scores (-34.5 and -27.5) are extreme, but they rest on 1 and 4 proteins, so they are not a meaningful measure of loading. Correlation to other channels is NaN for every channel, which follows from the missing channels (no protein is quantified in all channels), so no channel can be checked for correlation.
- Evidence: n_quantified: ch1=465, ch2=1, ch3=468, ch4=4, ch5=468, ch6=468. raw_median: ch2=11.363, ch4=13.187 vs 20.264-20.587 in the other channels. raw_median_z: ch2=-34.490, ch4=-27.485. corr_to_others is NaN for all channels. Parameters: reporter_mass_shift=0.002, isotope_correction=false, plex_corr_matrix_file=null, min_reporter_intensity=0.0, min_precursor_purity=0.0, -type tmt6plex, quantification step run on a single raw file with 1 fraction.
- Likely causes:
  1. Reporter-ion extraction tolerance too tight or mass-calibration offset, so reporter peaks for some channels fall outside the window (inference)
     - Reasoning: reporter_mass_shift is 0.002 Da, a very narrow window for reporter ions from an LTQ Orbitrap Velos HCD run. A small systematic m/z offset or low-resolution reporter peaks could cause selective loss. The affected channels, 127 and 129, are both the 15N-containing TMT6 reporters (127.1248, 129.1315), whose mass defects differ from the other channels. This would explain losing only two channels. This is a hypothesis, not shown by the metrics.
     - How to check: In the raw/mzML MS2 spectra, inspect observed m/z of reporter peaks at 126.128, 127.125, 128.134, 129.131, 130.141 and 131.138 and compute the offset in Da/ppm. Rerun IsobaricWorkflow with a wider reporter_mass_shift (for example 0.01-0.02) and see whether ch2/ch4 counts recover. Check isobaricworkflow.log.
  2. Channel-to-label mapping or experimental design mismatch (SDRF/openms_design) or wrong plex type
     - Reasoning: If the label order in PXD000001.sdrf_openms_design.tsv does not match the channels extracted, some channels could be empty. The SDRF lists TMT126-TMT131 in order, which looks correct, but the generated design file was not shown. The 4 working channels would then reflect only correctly mapped labels.
     - How to check: Open PXD000001.sdrf_openms_design.tsv and confirm the channel-to-sample mapping (channels 1-6 to TMT126-131) and that -type tmt6plex matches the labelling. Verify the consensusXML contains all six channels with non-zero intensities per PSM.
  3. Reporter ions missing in the spectra themselves (acquisition or conversion problem, such as peak picking or filtering of the low-m/z region in the mzML, or a real labelling/sample failure for those channels)
     - Reasoning: Low-mass reporter peaks can be lost during conversion or by the instrument scan range or isolation settings. A true sample or labelling failure is less likely to hit exactly two channels and leave the rest uniform. The very low raw_median in these channels (11.4-13.2) suggests the few values present are near the noise floor.
     - How to check: Inspect raw MS2 spectra for reporter ion peaks at m/z 127.125 and 129.131 directly in the raw file. Compare the unprocessed mzML with the raw file. Check the labelling efficiency (TMT6plex on K and N-term, fixed mod in SDRF) and the sample prep records for samples 2 and 4.
- Recommendation: Do not use ch2 and ch4 for any quantitative interpretation. Investigate reporter-ion extraction first: check the observed reporter m/z offsets, widen reporter_mass_shift and rerun, and verify the design file channel mapping. If channels remain empty, inspect reporter ions in the raw spectra.

### 2. [minor] ch1, ch3, ch5, ch6
- Observation: Spike-in slopes are computed only on the four channels that were quantified. Because ch2 and ch4 are missing, they omit the intermediate and extreme levels (for example ENO1 at 5 and 1, ALBU at 2.5 and 10), so the slopes are only partially informative. Several are below 1, showing some ratio compression, while the CYC slope is above 1 but rests on only 3 PSMs. No spike-in clearly fails, but recovery cannot be fully assessed.
- Evidence: slope: ENO1_YEAST 0.882 (31 psms, expected_range_log2 3.322), ALBU_BOVIN 0.740 (31 psms, 3.322), PYGM_RABIT 0.753 (48 psms, 1.000), CYC_BOVIN 1.156 (3 psms, 1.000). Per the SDRF, the four remaining channels span ALBU quantities 1,5,5,1 and PYGM 2,2,1,1.
- Likely causes:
  1. Typical TMT ratio compression from co-isolation interference, plus a reduced set of channels with a narrower dynamic range
     - Reasoning: MS2-based reporter quantification commonly compresses ratios, and isotope_correction=false and min_precursor_purity=0.0 mean no purity filtering or isotope impurity correction is applied. Slopes of 0.74-0.88 are consistent with this (inference).
     - How to check: Apply min_precursor_purity filtering and isotope correction (supply a plex_corr_matrix_file), then recompute slopes. Compare the effect on the spike-in slopes.
  2. Noise from a small number of PSMs or peptides per spike-in (especially CYC with 3 PSMs) and from the missing channels
     - Reasoning: Slopes from 3 PSMs and 4 data points are unreliable, and 1.156 may be noise rather than over-recovery.
     - How to check: Check peptide-level reporter intensities for the spike-ins. Recompute the slopes after ch2/ch4 are recovered.
- Recommendation: Re-evaluate spike-in recovery after fixing ch2 and ch4, using all six channels. Consider isotope correction and a precursor purity filter to reduce compression.
